Dear Editors of Empirical Software Engineering,

Please consider “Did the Tests Run? Measuring Fresh Test Execution in Coding-Agent Traces” as an empirical research article.

Researchers often use test commands and named passing cases in agent logs as evidence that the agent exercised a patch. We measured when that inference is possible. In the first 15 rounds of 46,834 Go-labeled trajectories, 295 of 5,925 recognized package successes explicitly replayed cached results and another 1,569 reported no matching tests. Across eight packages in Go's standard library, an independent test-body counter distinguished fresh execution from verbose replay even though both printed the named case as passing.

The paper also quantifies sensitivity to the interpretation of the logs. The weighted command–execution evidence gap in the detector-positive audit frame remained at least 15.61 percentage points over all itemwise choices between the two first-round judgments. We disclose the AI-assisted origin of the reviews and the automated adjudication. The primary held-out prediction contrast was small and its interval crossed zero.

The public companion repository supplies the extraction and instrumentation code, source checksums, and sufficient aggregate data for checking the main results. Appendix A explains the overlap with a prior repository manuscript on a single failure marker.
